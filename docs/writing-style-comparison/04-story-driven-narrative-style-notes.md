# Style 4: Story-driven narrative

## Essence

This style turns an abstract process into a sequence the reader can mentally
follow. It uses concrete participants, movement, place, and change to make the
mechanism memorable while periodically stepping out of the story to state the
exact science.

## What is different

- The opening begins with a scene rather than a definition.
- A photon, water molecule, and carbon atom provide continuity.
- Scientific structures become locations in a chronological journey.
- Misconceptions are corrected at the moment the story reaches the relevant
  event.
- Environmental factors alter the unfolding journey.
- A final boundary explains that the narrative is a tracking device, not a
  claim that molecules have intentions.

The organizing question is "What sequence of concrete changes will make this
process vivid and easy to remember?"

## Candidate skill rules

An eventual skill based on this style would instruct the agent to:

1. Choose a concrete perspective that naturally traverses the subject.
2. Build a chronological or spatial sequence with clear continuity.
3. Keep every narrative event faithful to the real mechanism.
4. Introduce technical terms where the story encounters them.
5. Step out of the narrative for important distinctions and limitations.
6. Avoid giving objects unsupported thoughts, goals, or human motives.
7. End by restating the precise model beneath the story.

## Chat behavior

In chat, this voice is vivid and engaging. It is useful when the user wants an
intuitive explanation, when the process unfolds over time, or when memory and
interest matter as much as compression.

It should not invent a story for a simple fact or for a high-stakes instruction
where narrative would hide the critical action.

## Strengths

- Memorable and easy to visualize mentally.
- Gives continuity to a process with many stages.
- Makes unfamiliar terms feel situated rather than arbitrary.
- Can sustain attention through a longer explanation.

## Tradeoffs

- Longer and less efficient for lookup.
- Narrative language can blur causal or quantitative precision.
- Readers may remember the journey but forget formal terminology.
- Poorly controlled personification can create false beliefs.

## Best fit

Choose this as the base style if you value engaging explanations, remember
sequences through stories, and want the agent to make abstract subjects feel
concrete.

## Evaluation signal

This candidate is a strong match if you can replay the journey later and use it
to reconstruct the science. It is a weak match if the narrative feels like a
detour from the information.
