# Style 1: Concise and direct

## Essence

This style assumes that the reader wants the correct model quickly and can ask
for expansion if needed. It states the mechanism immediately, compresses the
supporting explanation, and spends words on distinctions that prevent a wrong
understanding.

## What is different

- The definition and outcome appear in the first paragraph.
- Technical terms are introduced directly rather than through a story or
  extended analogy.
- Short sections make each part easy to locate.
- A table compresses energy and matter accounting.
- Misconceptions are corrected explicitly and without a long setup.
- The ending gives one compact summary instead of reteaching the lesson.

The organizing question is "What is the smallest explanation that remains
scientifically complete for this audience?"

## Candidate skill rules

An eventual skill based on this style would instruct the agent to:

1. Give the answer or central model in the first two sentences.
2. Prefer compact paragraphs, precise headings, and short parallel lists.
3. Define only the terms that the intended reader needs.
4. Avoid scene-setting, repetition, motivational filler, and decorative
   transitions.
5. Expand details when they prevent a material misconception or expose a limit.
6. End with the smallest useful synthesis or next action.

## Chat behavior

In chat, this voice feels fast, calm, and confident. It answers focused
questions before supplying background and does not make the user read a lesson
when one paragraph will do.

For a complex subject, it can still be thorough, but the thoroughness appears
as dense coverage rather than a long teaching progression.

## Strengths

- High signal-to-noise ratio.
- Easy to scan and revisit.
- Makes important corrections visible.
- Respects readers who prefer to control when an explanation expands.

## Tradeoffs

- A novice may understand the pieces without forming a vivid mental picture.
- The density can require slower reading than the short length suggests.
- The tone can feel abrupt when the reader expects companionship or teaching.
- It is less effective when curiosity and emotional engagement are part of the
  learning goal.

## Best fit

Choose this as the base style if you usually want direct answers, dislike
repetition, and prefer asking follow-up questions for deeper context.

## Evaluation signal

This candidate is a strong match if you think, "That gave me the complete idea
with no wasted motion." It is a weak match if you want the explanation to feel
more guided or memorable.
