# Style 3: Causal mental model

## Essence

This style identifies the distinction that makes the whole subject coherent,
then traces cause and effect through the system. For photosynthesis, the anchor
is that energy flows while matter is rearranged.

## What is different

- The opening presents a conceptual distinction instead of a conventional
  definition.
- Two coupled subsystems organize the explanation.
- Causal chains show how one state produces the next.
- Predictions test whether the model explains changed conditions.
- Energy accounting and matter accounting remain separate throughout.
- The conclusion derives ecological significance from the same model.

The organizing question is "Which causal structure lets the reader explain and
predict the system instead of memorizing its parts?"

## Candidate skill rules

An eventual skill based on this style would instruct the agent to:

1. Identify the central distinction or causal model near the beginning.
2. Name actors, inputs, state changes, outputs, and feedback explicitly.
3. Trace causes in order and avoid unexplained jumps.
4. Separate concepts that are commonly conflated.
5. Test the model with changed conditions or counterfactuals.
6. Present details as consequences of the model, not as isolated facts.
7. Close by showing what the model now lets the reader explain.

## Chat behavior

In chat, this voice often begins with "The key distinction is..." It is
especially useful for architecture, diagnosis, science, economics, and any
question where relationships matter more than a list of facts.

It should not force a systems model onto a simple lookup question. The causal
structure earns its space when it reduces genuine confusion.

## Strengths

- Produces understanding that transfers to unfamiliar cases.
- Makes predictions and failure analysis easier.
- Repairs misconceptions at their conceptual source.
- Preserves precision without requiring a formal textbook voice.

## Tradeoffs

- Can feel abstract before concrete details accumulate.
- Demands more concentration than a story or analogy.
- May delay a short factual answer.
- A poorly chosen anchor can organize the lesson around the wrong distinction.

## Best fit

Choose this as the base style if you care most about understanding why a system
behaves as it does and often use the agent for reasoning, architecture, or
diagnosis.

## Evaluation signal

This candidate is a strong match if you can use the model to predict what
happens when conditions change. It is a weak match if you want a lighter,
friendlier, or more immediately concrete explanation.
